3
Why Sagas exists

When people move on, stories can disappear with them.

Sagas keeps the memories connected to a place, so a person who comes looking later can still find them. Every story stays visible with the records and sources that help someone understand where it came from.

4
Where a story belongs

Sagas helps a story stay connected to a place, while an archive can keep it permanently.

Sagas is built around places, so it can turn up the people, records, and leads connected to a story. If you want material kept permanently, an archive may be the right place to donate it. We plan for contributors to choose what stays public and to be able to take something out of public view.

Planned behavior, not a live feature.

5
How stories are looked after

Memories stay visible as memories, with the sources behind them.

Sagas is designed to keep each memory connected to its source. When people remember something differently, the different versions stay visible with their own sources. Contributors choose what is public. These are the project's principles, not a promise that moderation or review is already available.

Project direction, not a live moderation feature.

6
Who runs the project

Sagas is a free project, and nothing on it is sold.

Sagas is run by the team building the project as a free public-interest archive. Nothing people contribute is sold. This draft does not name an organization, university, course, or person.

Draft wording, pending sponsor approval.

The first component set covers the map pin, site list item, narrative header, memory with its source, competing detail versions, and a record showing who brought it. Each component needs empty, loading, long-name, and disagreement states.

Where Sagas stands

This is an early project, and the site is not open yet.
